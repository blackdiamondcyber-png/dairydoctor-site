Herds that draw their own samples

The lab was built for operations that sample on their own schedule and mail the tubes. That covers more kinds of herds than most people expect.

  • Beef cow-calf herds

    Check at weaning or a month after the bull comes out, and cull on the answer instead of wintering an open cow.

  • Dairies without a weekly herd check

    Your own crew draws on your schedule. The open list is back before the next protocol day.

  • Custom heifer growers

    Heifers test from 25 days after breeding. Off-site heifers get checked without a vet visit to the lot.

  • Goat and sheep dairies

    Wisconsin milks more goats than any other state. Goats and sheep test from 30 days after breeding on the same blood test.

  • Bison ranches

    The assay is validated in bison, and nobody wants to palpate one. Draw at the handling system, mail the tubes.

  • Large herds

    Heifer checks, BVD PI screening of every calf, disease monitoring, and Dairy Comp 305 records review with the veterinarian.

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Collect samples

    Draw a blood sample from the tail vein (goats and sheep: the jugular vein) with a new needle for each animal. See the tail bleeding how-to guide.

  2. Step 2

    Fill out the right form

    First box? Please call our office before your first submission, at (715) 653-2201, or send the new client form. A call settles which supplies you need.

    Download the form that matches your samples:

    Cattle and bison form (PDF) Goat and sheep form (PDF) BVD PI form (PDF)

  3. Step 3

    Ship by midweek

    Pack your samples and form well, and ship so they reach our lab no later than Wednesday. Full shipping instructions here.

  4. Step 4

    Results Thursday

    Samples that reach the lab by Wednesday are reported Thursday by email, fax or phone, whichever you mark on the form. That is in time to put open animals on next week's breeding list.

When can I test?

Cows and bison from 28 days after breeding, virgin heifers from 25, goats and sheep from 30.

The vet reads your results

Samples that reach the lab by Wednesday are reported Thursday by email, fax or phone, whichever you mark on the form.

What Thursday's report shows

  • PregnantProtein present on the day she was sampled.
  • OpenNo protein. She can go back on next week's breeding list.
  • RecheckThe reading fell between the two ranges. It can point to a pregnancy in trouble, an animal sampled too early, or a cow still too close to calving.

What the veterinarian adds

  • Every plate is read by the lab's veterinarian, Dr. Glenn Pearson.
  • A recheck usually comes with a note on when to sample her again.
  • Every testing client gets Dr. Pearson's troubleshooting and support with their results. He is only an email or phone call away.

What the lab does for your herd

Pregnancy testing

BioPRYN blood pregnancy testing finds open animals with over 99% accuracy (BioTracking's published figure). Cattle from 28 days after breeding, heifers from 25, goats and sheep from 30.

BVD PI testing

Find and remove persistently infected (PI) animals before they cost your herd in scours, pneumonia and lost calves. Screen once, then test every calf and every purchase.

About Advanced Dairy Diagnostics & Consulting

Dr. Glenn Pearson, DVM has practiced dairy and beef production medicine for more than 40 years. He opened the practice in 1999 and in 2005 made it the first BioPRYN affiliate lab east of the Rockies. Every result comes from a lab built and overseen by a working dairy veterinarian.

Our lab is closed to the public. For the biosecurity of every herd we test, we do not allow visitors into the laboratory. Samples come to us by mail.
